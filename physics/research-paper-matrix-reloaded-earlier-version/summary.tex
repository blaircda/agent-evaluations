\noindent There are 12 reports: three models (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus 5.5), two effort levels (low, high), and two runs each.

\section*{Counts}

\paragraph{Principal achievement.} All 12 reports give the same answer: the near-horizon (Maldacena/IMSY) limit in the bulk is the \emph{same} BPS/M$p$T decoupling limit that defines matrix theory at asymptotic infinity. This unifies matrix theory, AdS/CFT, non-Lorentzian holography and $T\bar T$. Reports differ mainly in how much weight they give the $p$-brane $T\bar T$ flow equations. Six reports call them the most concrete or checkable result (Opus high~1, Opus high~2, Sonnet high~1, Sonnet high~2, Sonnet low~2, Haiku high~2). Three reports checked the flow equation numerically: Haiku high~1 ($p=1$), Opus high~1 ($p=2$--$5$) and Opus high~2 ($p=0$--$5$).

\paragraph{Principal drawback.} The reports nearly agree here. All 12 note that the non-Lorentzian bulk geometries are not shown to solve the M$p$T supergravity equations of motion and torsional constraints. They differ in which point they lead with:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.75\textwidth}c}
\toprule
Headline drawback & Count\\
\midrule
Claims are kinematic: the DLCQ$^n$/DLCQ$^m$ conjecture is bookkeeping, and no observable is matched & 8\\
New bulk geometries are unverified (no EOM check, ad hoc $\ell$/$N$ rescaling) & 3\\
The ``intrinsic'' $T\bar T$ generation of the bulk is not established ($\Omega(r)$ is put in by hand, $\lambda$ is position-dependent) & 1\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Next direction.} The reports disagree most here.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.75\textwidth}c}
\toprule
Proposed follow-up & Count\\
\midrule
Formulate M$p$T supergravity and verify the backgrounds (plus isometries) & 6\\
Quantitative test of one DLCQ$^1$/DLCQ$^2$ pair (symmetries, protected spectrum) & 3\\
Isometry algebra of a single bulk geometry vs.\ boundary symmetries & 1\\
Position-dependent $T\bar T$ deformation at $p=1$ (F1/NS5) & 1\\
Non-abelian (and supersymmetric) $p$-brane $T\bar T$ flow & 1\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The non-abelian flow is the usual runner-up: about eight reports describe it as higher impact but lower feasibility.

\noindent Sonnet gives the most uniform answers: all four reports propose building M$p$T supergravity. Opus is the only model that proposes testing a concrete pair with matched observables. Two of its reports choose the D4/M0T example, and Opus high~2 shows that it lifts exactly to null-compactified AdS$_7\times S^4$. Haiku's answers vary the most.
